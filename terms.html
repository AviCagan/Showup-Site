Terms of use

Last updated: 22 September 2026

Short, because the app is simple: it logs what you do and, if you want, lets a few people you chose see how often you show up. These terms cover the app and its social features. The privacy policy covers your data.

1. Who can use ShowUp

  • You sign in to use ShowUp, with email or Google, so the app is for people 16 and older. Give your real birth month and year. You accept these terms and the privacy policy when you create your account.
  • This is an alpha build for testers: features change between builds, some are unfinished, and a build may ask you to start over. Export your data from Settings whenever you want a copy.
  • One account per person. Keep your password to yourself.
  • Your username and display name must not impersonate someone else or contain slurs or harassment. Names that do are refused or removed.

2. Not medical advice

ShowUp estimates training coverage and energy balance from what you log. Colors on the body mean how well your week's sets cover a muscle, never how big it is. Ares explains your numbers and answers questions; in chat it may suggest loads, sets, reps and calories, always inside ranges the app works out from your own logs, and it may quote general facts from a short list of health sources. It is not a coach, doctor or dietitian and it does not diagnose or treat anything. Never train through pain. The wellbeing check-in points to mental-health resources when your own logs show a pattern worth a conversation; it is an offer of help, not a diagnosis. Talk to a doctor or a qualified professional before changing how you train or eat if you have a condition, are pregnant, or are under medical care. You train at your own risk.

3. Your content

What you share stays yours. By sharing a workout, posting a comment, creating a challenge or event, or sending a message, you let us store it and show it to the people you chose, and only them, for as long as the privacy policy says. Share only what you have the right to share. Nothing in the social features can carry a photo.

Two things you type or send to Ares are handled once and not kept: a question about an exercise, answered from that exercise's own description in the app's catalog, and a meal description or meal photo, used only to name the foods and portion sizes in it. Neither is stored on our server. Ares never produces a number; food estimates are suggestions you can edit before they are logged, and they come from the food database on your phone.

  • post other people's personal information, such as phone numbers or addresses;
  • send spam or links (messages and comments may contain no links);
  • scrape data, probe other accounts, or work around rate limits and blocks.
